• Growing up, music was a calling for me. It was home.
  • I was a troubled kid. The cops were called on me in third grade while having a breakdown in elementary school. I didn’t hurt anyone, but the principal saw me as a threat. She didn’t know how to treat children.
  • When I was eleven, I thought I found solace in someone I idolized, who let me into their group after I jumped for joy at the prospect of meeting them. Their group has left scars on me I still have not recovered from. I was verbally abused and had my social life dangling over my head time and time again. People with good hearts were shamed and dismissed, and I was celebrated for taking part.
  • Eleven years later, I find that scared, traumatized and vindictive child continuing to scream out for help in my music, and I find it difficult to listen to it, knowing that it has taken so long to look back, and I have allowed this cycle to repeat itself time and time again. I find myself panicking, gasping for air when I listen to a single song of mine these days. I can’t bring myself to listen to much of anyone’s music anymore.
  • Home has fallen apart, and has left me broken and beaten down.
  • I don’t know who I am. I don’t know who I am supposed to be. I think I’ve lost track of the journey I was supposed to embark on in order to find that here. Half of my life has been stolen from me.
  • I have accepted that this life isn’t mine anymore. I would like to turn the page on my own accord. In whatever life I happen to find, I simply wish to be someone’s daughter, and someone’s friend. That’s all I need to be for now.
  • This extended play will be my last.
  • ‘Something In The Water’, the second single from my extended play, To Carry You Home, is out now on streaming services.
